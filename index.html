Before You Fly

Get data before you land

Many visitors from outside the EU arrive without mobile data. Set up a European eSIM before departure so you can navigate to the apartment, buy metro tickets in the DSB app and message us as soon as you land. Airalo is one option, with plans from about USD 5. eSIMs work on iPhone XS and newer and most recent Android phones.

Apps to download

  • DSB – metro, bus and train tickets. Ticket machines can be awkward, so it is easier to have the app ready.
  • Wolt – food delivery.
  • Citymapper or Google Maps – for getting around.

Power adapters

Denmark uses Type K sockets, and Type E/F European plugs also fit. Visitors from the US and UK will need an adapter. Bring one along, or pick one up at the 24/7 7-Eleven by the Marble Church.

What to pack

The weather can change quickly, so layers are always a good idea. From April to September, bring a light rain jacket. From October to March, pack a warm waterproof coat, hat, gloves and shoes with good grip—wet cobblestones get slippery. Good walking shoes are useful all year.

The apartment is up one flight of stairs and there is no lift, so packing light will make arrival easier. Shampoo, conditioner, body wash and laundry detergent are provided; they are all eco-certified.

Christmas & New Year

Danes celebrate Christmas on the evening of 24 December. Most shops and restaurants close from 24–26 December and on 1 January. Shops also close early on 24 and 31 December, so plan to shop the day before; the 24/7 7-Eleven is a handy backup. Many restaurants close for the first week or two of January.

On New Year's Eve, expect fireworks throughout the city.

Getting Here

By metro from the airport

Take the airport metro to Kongens Nytorv (about 12 minutes), then change to the M3 or M4 towards Østerport. Ride one stop to Marmorkirken (the Marble Church). The apartment is about 30 seconds from the station.

By train

Østerport station is a 10-minute walk away, with regional trains around Denmark and across to Sweden.

By taxi

The drive from the airport takes about 15 minutes and usually costs around DKK 250–400 (USD 35–60 / GBP 30–45). Use the official taxi rank at arrivals rather than drivers who approach you inside. All taxis take cards. For pre-booking, try TAXA 4x35 or Uber, which uses licensed taxis here.

Parking

Street parking is expensive. Q-Park Adelgade is a 5-minute walk away, or you can park more cheaply near the airport at PParkCPH and take the metro into town.

Your First Day

Morning · Buka Bakery

Walk 5 minutes to Buka Bakery at Esplanaden 14 for coffee and their salt-and-pepper bun with butter and cheese.

Late morning · Kastellet & the harbour

Walk north into Kastellet, the 17th-century star fortress, then follow Langelinie past the Little Mermaid. Head south along the harbour to Skuespilhuset (the Royal Playhouse), then make your way home. Allow 45–60 minutes at an easy pace.

Lunch · Smørrebrød or Italian

Try smørrebrød at Restaurant Amalie on Amaliegade, an 8-minute walk away. No booking is needed at lunch except in December. Or have Italian at Bottega Barlito on Esplanaden.

Afternoon · Check in

Check in from 15:00, unpack and make a coffee.

Late afternoon · Torvehallerne

Head to the food halls at Frederiksborggade 21 by metro via Kongens Nytorv, or take a 20-minute walk. At Slagter Lund, try the roast pork sandwich with crackling (flæskestegssandwich)—it is worth the queue and can double as dinner.

Evening · Take it easy

Pick up fresh pasta to take away from Fabro at Borgergade 134, just 2 minutes away. Eat at home or on the rooftop if the weather allows, then enjoy an early night.

Explore the Neighbourhood

MUST SEE & DO

The Marble Church
Our pick
1 min walk

The Marble Church is in our opinion the most beautiful church in Denmark. We married and baptised our daughter there. Book the tower tour (very limited hours) for a spectacular view of Copenhagen. If you just want to enjoy the dome from the outside — go up to our rooftop terrace with some drinks on a warm evening and watch the sun go down.

Directions
Tivoli Gardens
10 minutes by metro

An amazing amusement park like nothing else — the second-oldest still running, and locals keep coming back every season. You can easily spend your day eating at the great restaurants, going on the rides or just enjoying the atmosphere — a city in itself. In October it dresses up for Halloween, and from mid-November to early January it becomes the best Christmas market in the city — worth the entry on its own, and still open the week after the outdoor markets have packed up. Closed for a week or so in early November, and from early January to late March.

Directions
Copenhagen Zoo
About 20 minutes by metro and foot

We love taking our daughter to Copenhagen Zoo. Stock up on snacks, drinks, and lunch before going in (it's allowed!) and enjoy the beautiful enclosures, most of them designed by world-famous architects. The Giant Panda enclosure is something special — and you can book lunch or dinner at its restaurant, with only a thin pane of glass between you and the pandas.

Directions
Canal Boat Tours
Summer

Seeing Copenhagen from the harbour is one of the most memorable things you can do. The canals wind past Christiania, Islands Brygge, the Opera House, Nyhavn, and the Black Diamond — and the perspective from the water is completely different from walking.

GoBoat
Price: Around DKK 499/hour.

You captain your own small electric boat (no licence needed, max 8 people). Pack drinks and snacks, pick a route, and explore at your own pace. The most fun option for groups and couples.

Directions
HeyCaptain

A guided tour where a local skipper takes you around. More relaxed, and you'll learn about the city while cruising. Good range of different tours available.

Directions

Both depart from locations within 10–15 minutes of the apartment. GoBoat's Nyhavn departure point is closest. Book ahead in summer — they sell out quickly.

Harbour Swimming — Islands Brygge
Summer
15 minutes by bike or metro (change at Kongens Nytorv to the M1)Free

In summer, harbour swimming is one of the most Copenhagen things you can do. The water is clean, the facilities are free, and locals treat the harbour pools like their living room. The harbour bath at Islands Brygge (Havnebadet) is Copenhagen's most popular harbour bath, with diving platforms and a chilled-out atmosphere right on the water. Bring a towel to sit on, pack some food and drinks (alcohol is allowed in public spaces in Denmark — grab a few beers or a bottle of wine from Netto), and spend an afternoon. It gets busy on warm days, but that's part of the charm.

Directions
Winter Bathing — SaunaNor
Winter
Two stops on the metro going to Nordhavn

This is serious stuff in Denmark. All winter, a lot of the population voluntarily strips down to bathing suits and jumps into the harbour, even if there is ice. A fantastic experience that we enjoy (and do frequently). SaunaNor offers guest passes. It is a 50-minute sauna experience where after 10 minutes in the sauna (between 80–100°C / 175–212°F for our American guests), you will have a chance to cool yourself down in the harbour. Bring a towel, bathing suit, a bottle of water and bravery!

Directions
Dyrehaven (The Deer Park)
About 25 minutes (walk to Østerport, S-train to Klampenborg)

For an easy day-trip out of the city, walk to Østerport Station, take the S-train, and suddenly you're in a beautiful 17th-century deer park once reserved for the Kings of Denmark. You'll get close to the deer (still the only ones in Denmark the King has exclusive hunting rights to), see the Eremitage hunting lodge where royal hunting feasts were held, and stop for your own feast at one of the great restaurants in and around the park — we recommend Peter Lieps Hus. In winter it is better still — the deer stand out against the frost, Bakken is mostly shut, and you can walk for an hour without meeting anyone. Go early; the light is gone by four.

Directions
Changing of the Royal Guard
2 min walkFree

Every day at noon, the Life Guards march from their barracks near Rosenborg Castle to Amalienborg Palace for the changing of the guard — a ceremony that has been performed since the 17th century. It's a small but quietly moving reminder that Denmark is still a Kingdom, and that some traditions here go back hundreds of years. The guards march down Bredgade and sometimes through Store Kongensgade itself — the street the apartment is on — so you may hear them before you even know to look.

Tip: The guard departs their barracks at Gothersgade (near Rosenborg) at around 11:30 and arrives at Amalienborg at noon. Arrive on the palace square by 11:45 for a good spot. Or simply stand on Bredgade — or even outside the apartment on Store Kongensgade, as the procession sometimes comes right past the front door. Fewer crowds, more atmosphere. The ceremony varies: when the King is in residence, a full military band accompanies; other times it's drums and fifes only, or silence.

Directions
Christmas Markets
Winter
6 November to 21 December20 min walk

Copenhagen does Christmas properly. The outdoor markets run from early November until just before Christmas Eve. Start at Nytorv & Gammeltorv, the two old squares off Strøget — wooden stalls, gløgg and æbleskiver. Tivoli's is ultimately the best of them, but go on a weekday: at the weekend the whole of Danish family Copenhagen is there.

Directions
Julefrokost — the Danish Christmas Lunch
Winter
late November through December

The julefrokost is an institution and nothing like a quiet lunch. It runs all through December: herring to start, then the warm dishes, then cheese, with beer and aquavit throughout, and it lasts for hours. If you want one, book as far ahead as you can. The good places are full by late November and weekends are essentially impossible by then — a weekday sitting is your realistic chance. Palægade, Møntergade, Aamanns 1921 and Restaurant Amalie all do it properly.

Directions
Copenhagen Light Festival
Winter
late January to about 21 February

February is the hardest month here — dark by four, cold, and a long way past Christmas. The Light Festival exists precisely for that. Light installations appear along the harbour and the canals for about three weeks, all free and all outdoors, and they are best walked after dinner when it is properly dark. Start at the Black Diamond and follow the water. The dates move a little each year, so check before you set out.

Directions
Christiansborg Tower
20 min walkFree

The best view in Copenhagen, and it costs nothing. A lift takes you most of the way up the tower at Christiansborg, and from the top the whole city finally makes sense — the harbour, the spires, the copper roofs. It is better in winter than in summer: go around four o'clock, when the light is going and the city starts lighting up beneath you. Bring a hat, it is exposed up there.

Directions
Den Blå Planet — the Aquarium
About 25 minutes by metro

When the weather is against you, this is where we go. Northern Europe's largest aquarium sits out by the airport, 25 minutes on the metro, and it will absorb half a day without anyone noticing. The big ocean tank with the hammerheads is the one everyone remembers. Go in the morning — it fills up with Danish families after lunch, especially on a wet weekend.

Cash & cards

If you need cash, use the Sydbank ATM at Kongens Nytorv; nearby alternatives charge high fees. Denmark is largely cashless: contactless Visa and Mastercard work everywhere, so you do not need kroner. Check your bank's foreign transaction fee, often 1–3%.

Getting around

Marmorkirken (M3/M4) is 30 seconds away, and the metro runs 24/7. Buy tickets in the DSB app. The airport is about 15 minutes away via Kongens Nytorv. You can also walk or use the Donkey Republic or Lime bike apps.

When cycling, raise a hand before stopping, signal turns, keep right, do not block bike lanes and never ride against traffic. Store Kongensgade is one-way.

Local etiquette

There is no dress code, even at Michelin restaurants. Tipping is not expected; feel free to decline the card terminal prompt, or round up at most. Be on time and apologise if you are late. Restaurants often have a set table time, so book ahead where possible.

Safety

Copenhagen and Frederiksstaden are very safe, including at night. Keep an eye on your belongings around Strøget, Central Station and Nørreport. Weekend nights from 23:00–05:00 can get rowdy, including on the metro. Late at night, we would avoid Blågårds Plads, Christiania and the party area around City Hall Square.

Emergency: 112 · Medical helpline: 1813

Running & Walking Routes

Harbour & Kastellet · about 10 km / 50–60 min

From the door, head along Store Kongensgade to Kastellet. Run through the fortress to Langelinie, then follow the quay past the ships and the Little Mermaid. Add a few laps of the ramparts if you like, then return along the harbour through Amaliehaven, past Amalienborg and the Marble Church, and back home.

Park run · about 6 km / 30–35 min

Run past Rosenborg Castle and loop around Kongens Have, then continue past the National Gallery (SMK) and loop around Østre Anlæg. Head along Kalkbrænderivej and back down Store Kongensgade. Finish with a coffee at Original Coffee on the left.
